# Pre-registration: more seeds, more history, a fuller sweep (2026-11-14)

**Registered:** 2026-11-14, before any of the runs below. The commit that adds
this file precedes the commit that adds their results, and `git log` is the
timestamp. Only short smoke runs (2k steps, at most three seeds, on a separate
unpinned download) were made beforehand, to check that the code runs; their
numbers are not used.

## Why

An outside reading of the study (2026-11-14) left five open points:

1. **One test period.** Every headline number comes from one 60/40 calendar
   cut. §5c tested four periods, but on BTC-USD alone and with the PPO from
   before its repairs (§10a).
2. **The crypto verdict is borderline.** The same ten seeds gave p = 0.22 on one
   machine and p = 0.03 on another (§5d). With six coins, the sign-flip test
   cannot give a p below 2/2⁶ = 0.031, so "worse than holding" needs the agent
   behind on every coin, and seed noise in the per-coin means decides it.
3. **The hyper-parameter sweep is thin.** It used 3 seeds at 60k steps, with the
   PPO from before its repairs.
4. **The headline uses means, not the run-level statistics** now standard for
   deep RL (Agarwal et al. 2021).
5. **The crypto agent's beta is 0.00 and nothing explains it.**

## Common setup

The archived data snapshot of 2026-10-28 (`2026-10-28-fresh-seeds.SNAPSHOT.json`,
verified by hash before every run); the repaired PPO (v2.3.0 defaults); 150k
steps per run; the leak-free common calendar cut for crypto (§10c); runs in
`.github/workflows/rigor.yml` (job `depth`) on GitHub-hosted runners. Seeds
100–109 are part of the D1 seeds. On these runners they will not reproduce the
published per-seed numbers (§5d explains why), so D1 is a new run of the study,
not an extension of the published one.

## D1. Thirty seeds

`tools/real_significance.py --seeds 30 --seed-base 100` (seeds 100–129), both
markets. The primary test is unchanged: a paired permutation test of the
seed-averaged agent return against buy-and-hold across the tickers (10 stocks,
6 coins).

- **P1.1.** Stocks: the agent is worse than buy-and-hold (negative difference,
  p < 0.05).
- **P1.2.** Crypto: the agent is not better than buy-and-hold (no positive
  difference with p < 0.05).
- **Decision rule for crypto.** If the difference is negative with p < 0.05 (the
  agent behind on all six coins), the paper says "worse than holding". Otherwise
  it says "not distinguishable from holding", and also reports on how many coins
  the agent trails. No prediction is made about which applies.

## D2. Walk-forward over the whole basket

`tools/basket_walk_forward.py`: four folds on one calendar for every ticker.
The window that every ticker trades in is split so that its first 40% is the
first fold's training period and the remaining 60% forms four equal test blocks.
In fold k the agent is trained from scratch on every ticker's history before
the block (expanding window, scaler fit on those rows) and is scored on every
ticker over the block. That makes 10 seeds (100–109) per fold. The primary test
pools the folds: a paired permutation test of the seed-averaged agent return
against buy-and-hold across every (ticker, fold) pair, 40 for stocks and 24 for
crypto. The pairs are not independent: the same ticker appears in four folds,
and tickers in the same fold move together. The pooled p is therefore read as
descriptive alongside the per-fold results, not as a sharper test than D1.

- **P2.1.** Stocks: the pooled difference is negative with p < 0.05.
- **P2.2.** Crypto: the pooled difference is not positive with p < 0.05.
- **P2.3.** In no fold, in either market, does the agent beat buy-and-hold
  (positive difference, p < 0.05).

## D3. The hyper-parameter sweep, done properly

`tools/hyperparameter_sweep.py --seeds 10 --timesteps 150000`: the same nine
configurations (baseline, and each of four knobs moved either side of its
default), seeds 200–209, both markets.

- **P3.1.** In neither market does any configuration have a positive mean edge
  over buy-and-hold.

## D4. What the crypto policy does (no prediction)

D1 records, per ticker and seed on the test period: the mean position, the mean
absolute position, the share of days long, short and flat (|position| < 5% of
equity), the daily turnover, and the hit rate. The hit rate is the share of
non-flat days on which the position's sign matched the asset's move. Three
explanations for a beta near zero are stated in advance, and the data decide
between them:

- **H-a, mostly in cash:** flat on more than half the days.
- **H-b, long and short in equal measure:** long and short shares within 10
  points of each other, and a mean absolute position at least three times the
  absolute mean position.
- **H-c, direction at chance:** a hit rate between 0.48 and 0.52, so whatever
  the positions are, they carry no information about the next day.

These can hold together, and all three are reported as found.

## Reporting

Means are reported next to run-level statistics: the interquartile mean of the
per-seed basket returns with a 95% bootstrap CI, and the probability that a
single run beats buy-and-hold on its ticker, with a CI that resamples seeds
within each ticker. P1.1, P1.2, P2.1 and P2.2 form one family and are also
reported Holm-adjusted.

## What would change the paper

- P1.1 or P2.1 fails: the stock result depends on the seeds or on the period,
  and the abstract says so.
- P1.2, P2.2, P2.3 or P3.1 fails: an edge exists somewhere that the paper says
  does not. It is reported as found, and in the case of P3.1 it is re-run with
  fresh seeds before anything is claimed.

Every prediction is reported as held or failed.
