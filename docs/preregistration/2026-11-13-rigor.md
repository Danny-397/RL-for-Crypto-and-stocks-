# Pre-registration: does the study hold up to standard RL evaluation practice?

**Registered:** 2026-11-13, before any of the full runs below. The commit that adds
this file precedes the commit that adds their results; `git log` is the timestamp.
Only short smoke runs (≤ 25k steps, one seed) were made beforehand, to time the
code; their numbers are not used.

## Why

A reviewer applying current deep-RL evaluation practice (Henderson et al. 2018;
Agarwal et al. 2021; Patterson et al. 2023) would raise four objections the study
does not yet answer:

1. The PPO is written from scratch and has never been shown to work on a standard
   benchmark. "PPO finds no edge" cannot be told apart from "this PPO is broken".
2. Only one algorithm and one implementation are tested, so the claim is about
   this code, not about RL.
3. The trading agents are trained for 60k–150k steps. Nothing shows that more
   training would not have helped.
4. The stock basket was chosen in 2026 and is made of the decade's winners
   (NVDA, AAPL, MSFT, …), which inflates buy-and-hold.

There are also two smaller ones: results are summarised by means over seeds rather
than the robust statistics now recommended, and the paper reports many p-values
without a correction for multiple testing.

## Experiments

Code: `tools/ppo_benchmark.py`, `tools/trading_rigor.py`, `rl_trader/evaluation/rigor.py`.
Runs: `.github/workflows/rigor.yml` on GitHub-hosted runners.

### E1. Is the PPO correct? (benchmarks)

Pendulum-v1 (300k steps), InvertedPendulum-v5 (300k), HalfCheetah-v5 (1M),
Hopper-v5 (1M); seeds 0–9; actions rescaled to [−1, 1]. Three arms with the
trading study's PPO hyper-parameters, not tuned for these tasks:

- `ours`: this repository's PPO, exactly as the trading study uses it;
- `ours_tb`: the same, but an episode cut off by a time limit is bootstrapped from
  the value of the state where it was cut, rather than treated as ended;
- `sb3`: Stable-Baselines3 2.9.2 PPO with the same hyper-parameters.

Score: the mean of the last three evaluations, each 10 deterministic episodes.

**Predictions and decision rules**

- **P1.1 (it learns).** On every task, the 95% bootstrap CI of `ours`' IQM lies
  above the score of a uniformly random policy.
- **P1.2 (it matches the reference).** On every task, `ours` is not worse than
  `sb3`: the 95% bootstrap CI of mean(`ours`) − mean(`sb3`) does not lie entirely
  below zero. If it does on any task, that is reported as a defect in the
  implementation, investigated, and the trading results are re-examined.
- **P1.3 (time limits).** On the two tasks whose episodes always end by time
  limit (Pendulum, HalfCheetah), `ours_tb` scores at least as well as `ours`.
  No prediction is made for Hopper and InvertedPendulum, which mostly end by
  falling.

### E2. Other implementations and another algorithm (trading)

Same data, split, seeds and scoring as the pre-registered replication of
2026-10-28: the archived snapshot of that day
(`2026-10-28-fresh-seeds.SNAPSHOT.json`), seeds 200–209, a 60/40 chronological
split, each ticker scored on its held-out 40% against buy-and-hold. Arms:
`ours`, `ours_tb`, `sb3_ppo` (same hyper-parameters), `sb3_sac` (SB3's SAC,
default settings, same network size).

- **P2.1 (reproduction).** `ours` at 150k steps is the replication's own
  configuration. Its basket means should match the replication (stock −19.8%,
  crypto +26.7%); differences can come only from floating-point differences
  between machines and are reported as found.
- **P2.2 (no edge, any implementation).** At 150k steps no arm beats
  buy-and-hold in either market: no paired permutation test across tickers shows
  a positive difference with Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 (family: 4 arms × 2 markets).
  On stocks every arm's difference to buy-and-hold is negative.

### E3. Training budget

`ours`, `ours_tb` and `sb3_ppo` are trained to 1M steps (`sb3_sac` to 300k) and
scored at 25k, 50k, 100k, 150k, 300k, 600k and 1M steps. The PPO has no
learning-rate schedule, so a checkpoint at *n* steps is the same as a run of *n*
steps.

- **P3.1.** No budget gives `ours` a positive edge over buy-and-hold with a
  Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 (family: 7 budgets × 2 markets).
- **P3.2.** More training does not reliably improve held-out return: the IQM at
  1M steps is not above the IQM at 150k steps by more than the width of its CI.

If P3.1 fails, the paper's conclusion is wrong at the published budget and is
rewritten to say so.

### E4. Survivorship (hindsight-free universe)

The 30 members of the Dow Jones Industrial Average on the first day of the stock
data, in force 2015-03-19 to 2017-09-01 and chosen by S&P Dow Jones Indices at
the time, not by this project. United Technologies and DuPont have since merged;
their successor listings (RTX, DD) carry the history. Data from 2016-11-14 to
2026-08-28 (Yahoo, fetched 2026-11-13, `data/SNAPSHOT_pit.json`), SPY as the
market index only. `ours`, 150k steps, seeds 200–209.

- **P4.1.** Buy-and-hold on this universe returns less than on the study's basket
  over the held-out period, showing that the basket's selection inflated the
  benchmark.
- **P4.2.** The agent still does not beat buy-and-hold: the paired permutation
  test across the 30 tickers does not show a positive difference at p < 0.05.

### E5. Reporting

Every table gains the IQM with a 95% stratified bootstrap CI, alongside the mean.
Every p-value in the paper is listed in one family and adjusted with
Holm–Bonferroni. Conclusions that rely on a test which does not survive the
adjustment are restated.

## What would change the paper

- P1.1 or P1.2 fails: the trading results are suspect until the defect is found.
- P2.2, P3.1 or P4.2 fails: there is an edge the paper missed, and the abstract
  changes.
- P1.3 holds and `ours_tb` also differs from `ours` on the trading task: the
  published PPO had a real defect, and the fix is reported with its effect.

Whatever the outcome, every prediction is reported as held or failed.
